# Biology Higher Paper 1: Mock 1

Original questions in the style of AQA GCSE Biology (8461) Higher Paper 1 (topics B1–B4). Not a real past paper.

**Time:** 1 hour 45 minutes · **Total:** 100 marks · **Calculator:** allowed

> You may use a calculator. Answer all the questions. Show your working in calculations: you can get marks for method even if your final answer is wrong. Questions marked 6 marks are extended answers: write in full sentences and organise your ideas. Diagrams and graphs are described in words; you can describe any diagram you would draw.

## Questions

### 1. (9 marks)

This question is about cells and microscopes.  
  
(a) Give two ways in which a bacterial cell is different from an animal cell. (2 marks)  
  
(b) A student looks at a cheek cell using a light microscope. In the student's drawing, the cell is 15 mm wide. The magnification is ×250.

(c) Write your answer to (b) in metres, in standard form. (1 mark)

### 2. (6 marks)

This question is about cell division.  
  
(a) Describe what happens in a cell during the stage of the cell cycle before it divides by mitosis. (2 marks)  
  
(b) A student looks at 200 cells in a slide of an onion root tip. 16 of the cells are dividing by mitosis. The whole cell cycle in these cells takes 20 hours.  
  
The proportion of cells dividing by mitosis is the same as the proportion of the cell cycle spent in mitosis. Calculate how long the cells spend in mitosis. Give your answer in minutes. Show your working. (3 marks)  
  
(c) Give one reason why mitosis is important to multicellular organisms. (1 mark)

### 3. (10 marks)

A student investigates osmosis in potato. She puts potato cylinders into sucrose solutions of different concentrations for 24 hours. She measures the mass of each cylinder at the start and at the end.  
  
Her results:  
0.0 mol/dm³: start 2.50 g, end 2.80 g, change +12.0%  
0.2 mol/dm³: start 2.48 g, end 2.60 g, change +4.8%  
0.4 mol/dm³: start 2.52 g, end 2.47 g, change −2.0%  
0.6 mol/dm³: start 2.50 g, end 2.30 g, change X  
0.8 mol/dm³: start 2.46 g, end 2.15 g, change −12.6%  
  
(a) Calculate the percentage change in mass, X, for the cylinder in 0.6 mol/dm³ sucrose solution. Show your working. (2 marks)  
  
(b) Explain why the student calculated the percentage change in mass, not just the change in mass. (1 mark)  
  
(c) Estimate the concentration of sucrose solution that has the same concentration as the potato cells. Explain how you got your answer. (2 marks)  
  
(d) Explain why the cylinder in 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose solution lost mass. (3 marks)  
  
(e) Root hair cells take up mineral ions from the soil by active transport. Explain why the cells must use active transport, and what it needs. (2 marks)

## Mark scheme

### 1. Microscopy and magnification (9)

- (a) First difference, e.g. a bacterial cell has no nucleus (its genetic material is free in the cytoplasm as a single loop of DNA)
- (a) Second difference, e.g. a bacterial cell has a cell wall, may have plasmids, or is much smaller
- (b) Rearranges: real size = image size ÷ magnification, 15 ÷ 250
- (b) 0.06 mm
- (b) 60 µm
- (c) 6 × 10⁻⁵ m
- (d) An electron microscope has a much higher resolution (and magnification) than a light microscope
- (d) So it can show much smaller structures in detail; ribosomes are too small to be resolved by a light microscope
- (e) Where aerobic respiration takes place (releasing energy for the cell)

*In (a), two different differences are needed; 'no mitochondria' is also acceptable. In (b), 0.06 with no unit or in mm scores 2 of the 3 marks. In (c), allow error carried forward from (b). In (d), 'it magnifies more' alone scores the first mark only. In (e), 'makes energy' scores 0.*

### 2. Mitosis and the cell cycle (6)

- (a) The DNA replicates / each chromosome is copied
- (a) The cell grows and the number of subcellular structures (such as ribosomes and mitochondria) increases
- (b) Proportion in mitosis: 16 ÷ 200 = 0.08
- (b) Time in mitosis: 0.08 × 20 = 1.6 hours
- (b) 96 minutes
- (c) Growth, repair (replacing damaged cells), or asexual reproduction

*In (b), 1.6 hours not converted scores 2 of the 3 marks. Allow 0.08 × 1200 minutes for the second and third marks.*

### 3. Diffusion, osmosis and active transport (10)

- (a) (2.30 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100
- (a) −8.0% (a decrease of 8%)
- (b) The cylinders had different starting masses, so percentage change allows a fair comparison
- (c) Use the concentration where the percentage change in mass would be zero (no net movement of water)
- (c) An estimate between 0.30 and 0.38 mol/dm³
- (d) The water concentration inside the potato cells was higher than in the solution (the solution was more concentrated)
- (d) So water moved out of the cells by osmosis
- (d) Through the partially permeable cell membrane, from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution
- (e) The concentration of mineral ions in the soil is lower than inside the root hair cell, so the ions must move against the concentration gradient
- (e) Active transport needs energy from respiration

*In (a), 8% without the minus sign or 'decrease' scores the method mark only. In (c), 'between 0.2 and 0.4' with the zero-change reasoning scores the first mark only. In (d), 'water moves from high to low concentration of water' is acceptable for the first idea; answers about sucrose moving into the cells score 0 for the second mark.*

### 4. Stem cells (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A balanced evaluation that compares the sources, with advantages and disadvantages including both practical and ethical points, and a reasoned conclusion.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** Some advantages and disadvantages of at least two sources, or a detailed account of one; a conclusion may be missing or not supported.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Simple statements about stem cells or their uses, with little evaluation.

Indicative content:
- Embryonic stem cells can differentiate into any type of cell, including insulin-producing cells
- Adult stem cells from bone marrow can only form a limited number of cell types, so may not be able to form pancreatic cells
- Using embryos raises ethical or religious objections, because the embryo is destroyed / has the potential to become a person
- Cells from a donor embryo may be rejected by the patient's immune system, so drugs to suppress the immune system would be needed
- Therapeutic cloning produces an embryo with the same genes as the patient, so the cells will not be rejected
- Therapeutic cloning is expensive and slow, and still involves creating and destroying an embryo
- Stem cells grown in the lab could be infected with viruses that could be passed to the patient
- There is a risk that stem cells divide uncontrollably and form tumours
- Treatment could cure the condition rather than needing insulin injections for life
- A conclusion weighing up the points

*Level 3 needs at least two sources compared and a conclusion. Answers that only describe what stem cells are stay in Level 1.*

### 5. The digestive system and enzymes (8)

- (a) Maltose (or sugars)
- (b) 1 ÷ 60 = 0.017 (accept 0.0167)
- (b) s⁻¹ (per second)
- (c) First control variable, e.g. the temperature
- (c) Second control variable, e.g. the volume or concentration of starch solution, or the volume or concentration of amylase
- (d) At pH 8 the shape of the active site of the amylase changes (the enzyme is denatured)
- (d) So starch no longer fits the active site as well
- (d) So fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form (per second)

*In (a), 'glucose' scores 0. In (c), 'the amount of' starch or amylase is acceptable; 'pH' scores 0 because it is the independent variable. In (d), 'the enzyme dies' or 'the enzyme is killed' scores 0.*

### 6. The heart, blood vessels and blood (7)

- (a) Blood enters the right atrium from the vena cava
- (a) It passes into the right ventricle
- (a) It leaves through the pulmonary artery to the lungs
- (b) Blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit of the body (one circuit to the lungs, one to the rest of the body)
- (c) Arteries have thick muscular and elastic walls
- (c) To withstand the high pressure of blood pumped out of the heart
- (d) It controls the natural resting heart rate (a group of cells in the right atrium)

*In (a), naming the left side of the heart scores 0 for that step; the order must be correct. In (c), the second mark must link the walls to the high pressure.*

### 7. Coronary heart disease and treatments (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A balanced evaluation that explains how each treatment works, gives advantages and disadvantages of both, and reaches a reasoned conclusion.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** Some advantages and/or disadvantages of both treatments, or a detailed account of one; a conclusion may be missing or not supported.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Simple statements about stents or statins, with little evaluation.

Indicative content:
- Fatty material narrows the coronary arteries, reducing the flow of blood (and oxygen) to the heart muscle
- Stents keep the coronary arteries open, so blood can flow to the heart muscle and the person can survive
- Stents work immediately and last a long time, and recovery from the operation is fairly quick
- Fitting a stent needs an operation, with a risk of complications such as infection, bleeding or blood clots
- Fatty material can build up again around a stent (the artery can narrow again)
- Statins reduce blood cholesterol, which slows down the build-up of fatty material
- Statins do not need an operation and may also reduce the risk of other cardiovascular problems
- Statins must be taken every day for life, take time to work, and can have side effects (e.g. muscle pain, liver problems)
- Statins cannot remove fatty material that is already there, so may not be enough on their own if an artery is badly blocked
- A conclusion, e.g. stents for a severe blockage, statins to prevent further build-up, or using both

*Level 3 needs both treatments evaluated and a conclusion.*

### 8. Plant tissues, xylem and phloem (7)

- (a) Volume of water taken up = 18 × 0.5 = 9 mm³
- (a) Rate = 9 ÷ 3 = 3 mm³ per minute
- (b) The rate increases
- (b) Moving air removes water vapour from around the leaf, so the concentration gradient is steeper and water evaporates and diffuses out of the leaf faster
- (c) They open and close the stomata (controlling gas exchange and water loss)
- (d) First difference, e.g. xylem transports water and mineral ions, phloem transports dissolved sugars
- (d) Second difference, e.g. xylem is made of dead cells strengthened with lignin, phloem is made of living cells; or water moves only upwards in xylem, sugars move both ways in phloem

*In (a), 6 mm per minute (no area) scores 0. In (b), the second mark needs the idea of removing water vapour or a steeper gradient, not just 'more wind'. In (d), the two differences must be different ideas.*

### 9. Viral, bacterial, fungal and protist diseases (7)

- (a) Viruses live and reproduce inside cells
- (a) This damages (or destroys) the cells
- (b) Eating food contaminated with the bacteria, e.g. undercooked chicken or food prepared in unhygienic conditions
- (b) The bacteria secrete toxins, which cause the symptoms (fever, stomach cramps, vomiting, diarrhoea)
- (c) First method, e.g. stop mosquitoes breeding by removing standing water
- (c) Second method, e.g. use insecticide-treated mosquito nets, or use insecticides
- (d) Antibiotics kill bacteria, but do not affect viruses (which live inside cells)

*In (c), the two methods must be different; also accept antimalarial drugs, or vaccination, as one method. In (d), 'measles is immune to antibiotics' scores 0.*

### 10. Antibiotics, drug development and monoclonal antibodies (8)

- (a) A placebo does not contain the drug, so the effect of the drug can be compared with no drug (to rule out the placebo effect)
- (a) Double blind means neither the patients nor the doctors know who has the drug and who has the placebo
- (a) This prevents bias in the results
- (b) A mouse is injected with an antigen, and lymphocytes that make the antibody for it are collected
- (b) The lymphocytes are combined (fused) with a tumour cell
- (b) To make a hybridoma cell, which can divide and make the antibody
- (b) A single hybridoma cell is cloned to make many identical cells, which all make the same antibody; the antibodies are collected and purified
- (c) A valid use, e.g. pregnancy tests, diagnosing diseases, measuring hormone levels, locating molecules in research, or treating some cancers

*In (a), 'so the patients don't know' alone does not score the second mark: the doctors must not know either. In (b), accept 'B lymphocytes' or 'white blood cells that make antibodies'.*

### 11. Plant disease and defences (4)

- (a) Less photosynthesis takes place (fewer leaves / less chlorophyll)
- (a) So less glucose is made for growth
- (b) A cellulose cell wall, a tough waxy cuticle on leaves, or layers of dead cells (bark) on stems
- (c) Yellow leaves (chlorosis)

*In (a), 'less food' is acceptable for glucose. In (b), chemical defences such as poisons score 0.*

### 12. Photosynthesis and limiting factors (10)

- (a) Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (with light)
- (b) Ratio of distances squared: (20 ÷ 10)² or (1 ÷ 10²) ÷ (1 ÷ 20²)
- (b) 4 times
- (c) Light intensity is no longer the limiting factor
- (c) Another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, is limiting the rate
- (d) A valid improvement, e.g. collect the gas in a gas syringe or measuring cylinder and measure its volume; or put a heat filter (beaker of water) between the lamp and the pondweed
- (d) A matching reason, e.g. the bubbles are different sizes so counting is not accurate; or the lamp also heats the water, so temperature would change
- (e) The heaters increase the temperature and the carbon dioxide concentration
- (e) In winter these are limiting factors, so the rate of photosynthesis increases
- (e) So more glucose is made, which the plants use for growth (e.g. to make proteins and cellulose)

*In (b), 2 times scores 0. In (d), the reason must match the improvement. In (e), the third mark needs the link from more glucose to growth.*

### 13. Aerobic and anaerobic respiration (6)

- (a) Glucose → ethanol + …
- (a) … + carbon dioxide
- (b) Anaerobic respiration transfers much less energy; or anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid, aerobic respiration produces carbon dioxide and water
- (c) The oxidation of glucose is incomplete
- (d) First example, e.g. converting glucose to starch, glycogen or cellulose
- (d) Second example, e.g. forming lipids from glycerol and fatty acids, making proteins from amino acids, or breaking down excess proteins to form urea

*In (a), lactic acid as a product scores 0 for the first mark. In (d), the two examples must be different reactions.*

### 14. Response to exercise and metabolism (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A clear and detailed explanation of changes during the race linked to the supply of oxygen and glucose, anaerobic respiration and lactic acid, and of the oxygen debt being repaid after the race, including what happens to lactic acid.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** An explanation of some changes during the race, with some links to respiration, and some reference to what happens after the race.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Simple statements about breathing or heart rate increasing, with little explanation.

Indicative content:
- Muscles contract more, so they need more energy from respiration
- Heart rate increases, and breathing rate and breath volume increase
- So more oxygenated blood reaches the muscles, and more carbon dioxide is removed
- The muscles cannot get enough oxygen, so they also respire anaerobically
- Anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid, which builds up in the muscles
- Muscles become fatigued and contract less efficiently
- After the race, breathing and heart rate stay high to repay the oxygen debt
- Blood carries lactic acid to the liver, where it is converted back into glucose
- Oxygen debt is the amount of extra oxygen needed to react with the lactic acid and remove it from cells

*Level 3 needs both the race and the recovery explained, including the liver or the oxygen debt.*
