# Physics Higher Paper 1: Mock 1

Original questions in the style of AQA GCSE Physics (8463) Higher Paper 1 (topics P1–P4). Not a real past paper.

**Time:** 1 hour 45 minutes · **Total:** 100 marks · **Calculator:** allowed

> You may use a calculator. Answer all the questions. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Equations you are not expected to remember are given in the question; all other equations you need to recall. Show your working in calculations: you can get marks for method even if your final answer is wrong. Give units with your answers where they are not given. Questions marked 6 marks are extended answers: write in full sentences and organise your ideas. Diagrams and graphs are described in words; you can describe any diagram you would draw.

## Questions

### 1. (9 marks)

A skateboarder of mass 60 kg starts from rest at the top of a ramp. The top of the ramp is 2.5 m above the bottom.  
  
(a) Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost by the skateboarder as they go down the ramp. Show your working. (2 marks)  
  
(b) Assume all of the gravitational potential energy lost is transferred to the kinetic energy store of the skateboarder. Calculate the speed of the skateboarder at the bottom of the ramp. Show your working. (3 marks)  
  
(c) The actual speed of the skateboarder at the bottom of the ramp is 6.0 m/s. Calculate the efficiency of the energy transfer from the gravitational potential energy store to the kinetic energy store. Show your working. (3 marks)  
  
(d) Explain why the actual speed is less than your answer to (b). (1 mark)

### 2. (6 marks)

A student wants to find the specific heat capacity of aluminium. She has a 1 kg aluminium block with two holes in it, an electric immersion heater, a thermometer, a joulemeter, a stopclock, a balance and some insulating material.  
  
Describe a method the student could use to determine the specific heat capacity of aluminium. Include how she should use her measurements to calculate it.  
  
The specific heat capacity is given by: change in thermal energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change (ΔE = m c Δθ).  
  
(6 marks)

### 2. Specific heat capacity (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A clear, logically ordered method that would produce valid results, including the measurements taken, how to reduce energy losses, and how to calculate the specific heat capacity from the measurements.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** A method with most of the key steps, which could produce results. Some idea of how the measurements would be used, but the calculation may be incomplete.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Some relevant steps, but the method would not produce valid results. Little or no idea of how to calculate the specific heat capacity.

Indicative content:
- Measure the mass of the block with the balance
- Put the heater in one hole and the thermometer in the other (a drop of water or oil in the thermometer hole improves contact)
- Wrap the block in insulating material to reduce energy transfer to the surroundings
- Record the starting temperature of the block
- Connect the heater to the power supply through the joulemeter (zeroed) and switch on
- Heat for a set time (e.g. 10 minutes) and record the energy transferred from the joulemeter
- Record the highest temperature reached (the temperature keeps rising briefly after switching off)
- Calculate the temperature change Δθ
- c = ΔE ÷ (m × Δθ)
- Alternatively, record temperature at regular intervals and plot temperature against energy; c = 1 ÷ (mass × gradient)

*Level 3 needs a workable method and a correct way to calculate c from the measurements.*

### 10. Atoms, isotopes and the development of the atomic model (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A detailed, logically ordered explanation of at least three stages in the model, each linked to the evidence that led to it, including the alpha particle scattering experiment.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** A description of at least two models with some explanation of the evidence, which may lack detail.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Simple statements about one or two models, with little or no evidence.

Indicative content:
- Discovery of the electron led to the plum pudding model: a ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it
- In the alpha particle scattering experiment, alpha particles were fired at thin gold foil
- Most alpha particles went straight through, so most of the atom is empty space
- Some were deflected and a very few bounced back, so the positive charge and most of the mass are concentrated in a tiny nucleus
- This led to the nuclear model, replacing the plum pudding model
- Bohr suggested electrons orbit the nucleus at specific distances (energy levels/shells); his calculations agreed with experimental observations
- Later experiments showed the positive charge of the nucleus is made of smaller particles: protons
- Chadwick's experiments about 20 years later provided evidence for neutrons in the nucleus

*Level 3 needs the alpha scattering evidence explained (not just described) and at least one later development.*
