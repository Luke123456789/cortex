# Maths Higher Paper 1 (non-calculator): Mock 1

Original questions in the style of Pearson Edexcel GCSE Maths (1MA1) Higher Paper 1. Not a real past paper.

**Time:** 90 minutes · **Total:** 80 marks · **Calculator:** not allowed

> You must not use a calculator. Answer all the questions. Show your working: marks are given for method, and a correct answer with no working may not get full marks. Diagrams are described in words; you can describe any diagram you would draw.

## Questions

### 1. (2 marks)

Work out 2¾ ÷ 1⅚  
  
Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form. Show your working.

### 2. (2 marks)

Work out (6 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁻²)  
  
Give your answer in standard form.

### 3. (3 marks)

A train travels 150 km in 1 hour 15 minutes.  
  
Work out the average speed of the train in km/h. Show your working.

### 4. (3 marks)

Amir and Beth share some sweets in the ratio 3 : 5.  
  
Beth gets 24 more sweets than Amir.  
  
How many sweets are there altogether? Show your working.

### 5. (4 marks)

(a) Expand and simplify (x + 5)(x − 3)  
  
(b) Factorise fully 6x²y − 9xy²

### 6. (3 marks)

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°.  
  
Work out the number of sides the polygon has. Show your working.

### 7. (3 marks)

Solve (3x − 1)/4 = x − 2  
  
Show your working.

### 8. (3 marks)

Work out an estimate for  
  
(4.87 × 61.2) ÷ 0.198  
  
Show your working.

### 9. (3 marks)

It takes 6 builders 10 days to build a wall. All the builders work at the same rate.  
  
(a) How many days would it take 4 builders to build the same wall?  
  
(b) The wall must be built in 5 days. How many builders are needed?  
  
Show your working.

### 10. (3 marks)

Sophie invests £2000 for 2 years at 5% per year compound interest.  
  
Work out the value of her investment at the end of 2 years. Show your working.

### 11. (4 marks)

There are 30 students in a class.  
18 study French.  
14 study Spanish.  
5 study neither French nor Spanish.  
  
A student is chosen at random. Work out the probability that the student studies both French and Spanish.  
  
You may describe a Venn diagram in words. Show your working.

### 12. (3 marks)

A straight line passes through the points (1, 4) and (5, 12).  
  
Find an equation of the line. Show your working.

### 13. (3 marks)

A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. The foot of the ladder is on horizontal ground, 2.5 m from the bottom of the wall.  
  
How far up the wall does the ladder reach? Show your working.

### 14. (4 marks)

Solve the simultaneous equations  
  
3x + 2y = 4  
5x − 3y = 13  
  
Show your working.

### 15. (4 marks)

A solid cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm.  
  
(a) Work out the volume of the cylinder.  
(b) Work out the total surface area of the cylinder.  
  
Give both answers in terms of π. Show your working.

### 16. (3 marks)

The recurring decimal 0.454545... (0.4̇5̇) can be written as a fraction.  
  
Use algebra to show that 0.454545... = 5/11

### 17. (3 marks)

(a) Work out the value of 27^(−2/3)  
  
(b) Work out the value of 81^(3/4)

### 18. (3 marks)

Make x the subject of the formula  
  
y = (2x + 3)/(x − 1)  
  
Show your working.

### 19. (4 marks)

(a) Write x² − 6x + 11 in the form (x − a)² + b, where a and b are integers.  
  
(b) Hence write down the coordinates of the turning point of the graph of y = x² − 6x + 11.  
  
(c) Explain how your answer to (a) shows that the equation x² − 6x + 11 = 0 has no real solutions.

### 20. (4 marks)

f(x) = 2x − 1 and g(x) = x²  
  
(a) Find fg(3).  
(b) Find f⁻¹(x).  
(c) Solve gf(x) = 25

### 21. (4 marks)

The table shows how long 38 people waited at a clinic, t minutes.  
  
0 < t ≤ 10: 8 people  
10 < t ≤ 15: 12 people  
15 < t ≤ 30: 18 people  
  
(a) A histogram is to be drawn for this data. Work out the frequency density for each class.  
  
(b) Estimate the number of people who waited between 12 and 20 minutes.  
  
Show your working.

### 22. (4 marks)

Two solid cones are mathematically similar.  
  
The surface area of the smaller cone is 20 cm².  
The surface area of the larger cone is 45 cm².  
The volume of the smaller cone is 16 cm³.  
  
Work out the volume of the larger cone. Show your working.

### 23. (3 marks)

OAB is a triangle. The vector from O to A is a and the vector from O to B is b.  
  
M is the midpoint of AB.  
N is the point on OB such that ON = ⅔ OB.  
  
Find the vector MN in terms of a and b. Give your answer in its simplest form. Show your working.

### 24. (5 marks)

There are n sweets in a bag. 6 of the sweets are orange.  
  
Sam takes a sweet at random and eats it. He then takes another sweet at random and eats it.  
  
The probability that Sam eats two orange sweets is 1/3.  
  
(a) Show that n² − n − 90 = 0  
  
(b) Work out the number of sweets that were in the bag at the start.

## Mark scheme

### 1. Fractions, decimals and percentages (2)

- Converts to improper fractions and multiplies by the reciprocal: 11/4 × 6/11
- 1½

*Accept 3/2 for the method mark only; the answer mark needs the mixed number 1½.*

### 2. Indices and standard form (2)

- 24 × 10³ or 24 000 seen
- 2.4 × 10⁴

*24 × 10³ (not in standard form) scores 1.*

### 3. Compound measures (3)

- Converts the time: 1 hour 15 minutes = 1.25 hours (or 75 minutes)
- Uses speed = distance ÷ time: 150 ÷ 1.25 (or 150 ÷ 75 × 60)
- 120 km/h

*Using 1.15 hours loses the first mark but can score the method mark.*

### 4. Ratio (3)

- The difference is 5 − 3 = 2 parts
- 1 part = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
- Total = 8 × 12 = 96

*Treating 24 as the total or as Beth's share scores 0.*

### 5. Expanding and factorising (4)

- (a) At least three of the four terms correct: x², −3x, +5x, −15
- (a) x² + 2x − 15
- (b) Takes out a correct common factor, e.g. 3x(2xy − 3y²) or xy(6x − 9y)
- (b) 3xy(2x − 3y)

*In (b) the final mark needs the fully factorised form.*

### 6. Angles and polygons (3)

- Exterior angle = 180° − 156° = 24°
- Number of sides = 360 ÷ 24
- 15 sides

*The algebraic method (n − 2) × 180 = 156n, giving 24n = 360, can score full marks.*

### 7. Linear and quadratic equations (3)

- Multiplies both sides by 4: 3x − 1 = 4(x − 2)
- Expands and collects terms, e.g. 3x − 1 = 4x − 8
- x = 7

*3x − 1 = 4x − 2 (not multiplying both terms by 4) loses the first mark; allow follow-through for the second.*

### 8. Bounds, rounding and estimation (3)

- Rounds each number to 1 significant figure: 5, 60 and 0.2
- 5 × 60 = 300, then 300 ÷ 0.2
- 1500

*Accept 1470 from 4.9 × 60 ÷ 0.2 if the rounding is shown. An exact answer (about 1505) with no rounding shown is not an estimate and scores 0.*

### 9. Direct and inverse proportion (3)

- Total work = 6 × 10 = 60 builder-days
- (a) 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days
- (b) 60 ÷ 5 = 12 builders

*Treating it as direct proportion (e.g. 6.7 days for 4 builders) scores 0 for that part.*

### 10. Percentage change, growth and decay (3)

- After 1 year: 2000 + 100 = £2100 (or 2000 × 1.05)
- After 2 years: 2100 + 105 (or 2100 × 1.05)
- £2205

*Simple interest (£2200) scores 1 for the first year only.*

### 11. Venn diagrams and set notation (4)

- 30 − 5 = 25 students study at least one of the languages
- 18 + 14 − 25 = 7 study both
- Correct Venn regions: French only 11, both 7, Spanish only 7, neither 5
- 7/30

*Max 4. 7 (a number, not a probability) as the final answer loses the last mark.*

### 12. Straight-line graphs (3)

- Gradient = (12 − 4) ÷ (5 − 1) = 2
- Substitutes a point: 4 = 2 × 1 + c, so c = 2
- y = 2x + 2 (or an equivalent equation)

*Allow error carried forward from a wrong gradient for the substitution mark.*

### 13. Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry (3)

- Uses Pythagoras with the ladder as the hypotenuse: h² = 6.5² − 2.5²
- = 42.25 − 6.25 = 36
- h = 6 m

*Adding the squares (6.5² + 2.5²) scores 0.*

### 14. Simultaneous equations (4)

- Makes the coefficients of one variable the same size, e.g. 9x + 6y = 12 and 10x − 6y = 26
- Adds or subtracts correctly to eliminate a variable, e.g. 19x = 38
- x = 2
- y = −1

*Substitution is an acceptable method. Trial and improvement with no algebra scores at most 1 mark.*

### 15. Area, volume and surface area (4)

- (a) Volume = π × 3² × 10
- (a) 90π cm³
- (b) Two circular ends = 2 × π × 3² = 18π and curved surface = 2 × π × 3 × 10 = 60π
- (b) 78π cm²

*Answers given as decimals (e.g. 282.7) instead of in terms of π lose the answer mark for that part.*

### 16. Fractions, decimals and percentages (3)

- Lets x = 0.4545... and writes 100x = 45.4545...
- Subtracts to get 99x = 45
- x = 45/99 = 5/11

*This is a 'show that' question: every step must be written down. Dividing 5 by 11 to get 0.4545... does not use algebra and scores 0.*

### 17. Indices and standard form (3)

- (a) Uses the cube root: 27^(1/3) = 3 (or 27^(2/3) = 9)
- (a) 1/9
- (b) 27

*In (a), 9 or −9 scores the first mark only.*

### 18. Inequalities and rearranging formulae (3)

- Multiplies both sides by (x − 1): y(x − 1) = 2x + 3
- Collects the x terms on one side: xy − 2x = y + 3
- Factorises and divides: x = (y + 3)/(y − 2)

*Accept equivalent forms, e.g. x = (−y − 3)/(2 − y).*

### 19. Quadratic and other graphs (4)

- (a) (x − 3)² seen
- (a) (x − 3)² + 2
- (b) (3, 2)
- (c) (x − 3)² is never negative, so x² − 6x + 11 is always at least 2 and can never equal 0 (or: the minimum point is above the x-axis)

*Allow error carried forward from (a) into (b).*

### 20. Functions and iteration (4)

- (a) fg(3) = f(9) = 17
- (b) f⁻¹(x) = (x + 1)/2
- (c) gf(x) = (2x − 1)², so 2x − 1 = 5 or 2x − 1 = −5
- (c) x = 3 or x = −2

*In (a), working out gf(3) = 25 (the wrong order) scores 0. In (c), both solutions are needed for the last mark.*

### 21. Histograms (4)

- (a) Uses frequency density = frequency ÷ class width
- (a) 0.8, 2.4 and 1.2
- (b) 12 to 15 minutes: 3/5 × 12 = 7.2; 15 to 20 minutes: 5/15 × 18 = 6
- (b) 13.2, so about 13 people

*Accept 13 or 13.2 for the final mark. Allow error carried forward from wrong frequency densities in (b).*

### 22. Congruence and similarity (4)

- Area scale factor = 45 ÷ 20 = 9/4
- Length scale factor = 3/2
- Volume scale factor = (3/2)³ = 27/8
- 16 × 27/8 = 54 cm³

*Using the area scale factor for the volume (16 × 9/4 = 36) scores 1.*

### 23. Vectors (3)

- AB = b − a
- OM = a + ½(b − a) = ½a + ½b
- MN = ON − OM = ⅔b − ½a − ½b = −½a + ⅙b

*Accept ⅙b − ½a or (b − 3a)/6.*

### 24. Tree diagrams and conditional probability (5)

- (a) P(first orange) = 6/n and P(second orange) = 5/(n − 1)
- (a) 6/n × 5/(n − 1) = 1/3
- (a) Rearranges: 90 = n(n − 1), so n² − n − 90 = 0
- (b) Factorises: (n − 10)(n + 9) = 0
- (b) n = 10, rejecting n = −9 because the number of sweets can't be negative

*(a) is a 'show that': the given equation must follow from the working. Using 6/n for both picks (with replacement) scores 0 for (a), but (b) can still score in full.*
