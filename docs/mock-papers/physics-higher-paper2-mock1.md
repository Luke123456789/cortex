# Physics Higher Paper 2: Mock 1

Original questions in the style of AQA GCSE Physics (8463) Higher Paper 2 (topics P5–P8). Not a real past paper.

**Time:** 1 hour 45 minutes · **Total:** 100 marks · **Calculator:** allowed

> You may use a calculator. Answer all the questions. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Equations you are not expected to remember are given in the question; all other equations you need to recall. Show your working in calculations: you can get marks for method even if your final answer is wrong. Give units with your answers where they are not given. Questions marked 6 marks are extended answers: write in full sentences and organise your ideas. Diagrams and graphs are described in words; you can describe any diagram you would draw.

## Questions

### 1. (7 marks)

A crate of mass 40 kg is on a horizontal floor. A worker pushes the crate with a horizontal force of 150 N. The friction force on the crate is 70 N.  
  
(a) Calculate the weight of the crate. Show your working. (2 marks)  
  
(b) Give the resultant force on the crate. (1 mark)  
  
(c) Calculate the acceleration of the crate. Show your working. (2 marks)  
  
(d) Two forces act on a different object: 30 N to the east and 40 N to the north. Determine the size of the resultant force on the object. Show your working. (2 marks)

(6 marks)

### 2. Forces and elasticity (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A clear, logically ordered method that would produce valid results, including how extension is measured accurately and how the results are processed (graph) to show the relationship.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** A method with most of the key steps that could produce results, with some idea of how to process them.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Some relevant steps, but the method would not produce valid results.

Indicative content:
- Hang the spring from the clamp, with the metre rule clamped vertically beside it
- Measure the original (unstretched) length of the spring, using the pointer on the rule
- Add a weight to the hanger and measure the new length
- Read the rule at eye level to avoid parallax error
- Extension = new length − original length
- Repeat, adding one weight at a time (e.g. up to 6 N)
- Take the readings again as weights are removed, or repeat and find a mean
- Plot a graph of force (y-axis) against extension (x-axis)
- A straight line through the origin shows extension is directly proportional to force (up to the limit of proportionality)
- The gradient gives the spring constant
- Safety: wear eye protection / avoid standing under the weights

*Level 3 needs extension calculated correctly from lengths and a graph used to find the relationship.*

### 6. Newton's laws of motion (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A detailed, logically ordered explanation of all stages of the fall, linking the changes in velocity to the size of the resultant force at each stage, including both terminal velocities.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** An explanation of at least two stages, with some link between forces and motion.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Simple statements about the forces or the motion, with little linking.

Indicative content:
- At first she accelerates downwards at about 9.8 m/s², because the only significant force is her weight
- As her speed increases, air resistance (drag) increases
- So the resultant force decreases and her acceleration decreases
- Eventually air resistance equals her weight; the resultant force is zero
- So she travels at a constant (terminal) velocity
- When the parachute opens, the surface area increases, so air resistance increases a lot
- Air resistance is now greater than her weight, so the resultant force is upwards and she decelerates
- As she slows, air resistance decreases until it equals her weight again
- She reaches a new, lower terminal velocity, which is safe for landing

*Level 3 needs both terminal velocities explained in terms of balanced forces.*

### 14. The solar system and the life cycle of a star (6)

- **Level 3 (5–6):** A detailed, correctly ordered description of every stage from nebula to neutron star or black hole, including how elements up to iron and heavier than iron are formed.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** A description of most stages in the correct order, with some reference to the formation of elements.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** Some stages named, possibly in the wrong order, with little detail.

Indicative content:
- A star forms from a cloud of dust and gas (nebula), pulled together by gravity
- This forms a protostar; it heats up as it gets denser
- When it is hot enough, hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium: fusion starts and it becomes a main sequence star
- On the main sequence the outward force (pressure) from fusion energy balances the inward pull of gravity, so it is stable for a long time
- When the hydrogen runs out, it swells to become a red super giant
- In the red super giant, fusion forms heavier elements, up to iron
- It explodes as a supernova
- Elements heavier than iron are formed in the supernova, and the explosion spreads elements throughout the universe
- What is left becomes a neutron star or, if the mass is great enough, a black hole

*Level 3 needs the stages in the correct order and both iron and heavier-than-iron element formation. Describing the Sun's life cycle (white dwarf) instead loses access to Level 3.*

### 15. Red-shift and the Big Bang (4)

- (a) An observed increase in the wavelength of light from distant galaxies (shifted towards the red end of the spectrum)
- (b) Light from almost all distant galaxies is red-shifted, so they are moving away from us
- (b) The further away the galaxy, the greater the red-shift, so the faster it is moving away
- (b) So the universe is expanding
- (b) Which suggests it began from a very small, extremely hot and dense region (the Big Bang)

*In (b), award up to 3 marks from the 4 points.*
