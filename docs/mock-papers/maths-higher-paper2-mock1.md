# Maths Higher Paper 2 (calculator): Mock 1

Original questions in the style of Pearson Edexcel GCSE Maths (1MA1) Higher Paper 2. Not a real past paper.

**Time:** 90 minutes · **Total:** 80 marks · **Calculator:** allowed

> You may use a calculator. Answer all the questions. Show your working: marks are given for method, and a correct answer with no working may not get full marks. If your calculator gives a long decimal, write down the full value before you round it. Diagrams are described in words; you can describe any diagram you would draw.

## Questions

### 1. (3 marks)

A laptop costs £640.  
  
In a sale, the price is reduced by 15%. After the sale, the sale price is increased by 15%.  
  
Ali says, "The laptop is now back to £640."  
  
Is Ali correct? You must show your working.

### 2. (3 marks)

Priya invests £4500 in a savings account.  
  
The account pays 3.2% compound interest per year.  
  
Work out the value of her investment at the end of 5 years. Give your answer to the nearest penny.

### 3. (3 marks)

Green paint is made by mixing blue paint and yellow paint in the ratio 3 : 5.  
  
Kim has 1.2 litres of blue paint and 1.8 litres of yellow paint.  
  
Work out the greatest amount of green paint Kim can make. Show your working.

### 4. (3 marks)

A metal block is a cuboid measuring 4 cm by 5 cm by 12 cm.  
  
The density of the metal is 8.9 g/cm³.  
  
Work out the mass of the block in kilograms.

### 5. (3 marks)

The table gives information about the times, t minutes, that 40 people took to finish a puzzle.  
  
| Time (t minutes) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0 < t ≤ 10 | 5 |
| 10 < t ≤ 20 | 12 |
| 20 < t ≤ 30 | 15 |
| 30 < t ≤ 40 | 8 |
  
Work out an estimate for the mean time. Show your working.

### 6. (2 marks)

The mass of the Earth is 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg.  
  
The mass of the Moon is 7.35 × 10²² kg.  
  
How many times greater is the mass of the Earth than the mass of the Moon? Give your answer to 3 significant figures.

### 7. (3 marks)

ABC is a right-angled triangle.  
  
Angle ABC = 90°. AB = 7.4 cm and BC = 11.2 cm.  
  
Work out the size of angle ACB. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place.

### 8. (4 marks)

A cylinder has radius 6 cm and height 15 cm. It is full of water.  
  
All of the water is poured into an empty tank shaped like a cuboid. The base of the tank measures 20 cm by 15 cm.  
  
Work out the depth of the water in the tank. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. Show your working.

### 9. (3 marks)

A, B, C and D are points on a circle, so ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral.  
  
Angle ABC = (3x + 10)° and angle ADC = (2x + 20)°.  
  
Work out the size of angle ABC. Give a reason for each stage of your working.

### 10. (3 marks)

y is inversely proportional to the square of x.  
  
When x = 5, y = 12.  
  
Work out the value of y when x = 8.

### 11. (3 marks)

A bag contains 5 red counters and 3 blue counters.  
  
Zac takes a counter at random and does not replace it. He then takes a second counter at random.  
  
Work out the probability that the two counters are different colours. Show your working.

### 12. (3 marks)

Here are the first five terms of a quadratic sequence.  
  
4   11   22   37   56  
  
Find an expression, in terms of n, for the nth term of the sequence.

### 13. (3 marks)

Line L passes through the points (2, 5) and (6, −3).  
  
Find the equation of the line that is perpendicular to L and passes through the point (4, 1).

### 14. (4 marks)

Make x the subject of the formula  
  
y = (3x + 2) / (x − 4)

### 15. (3 marks)

Solve 3x² − 7x − 4 = 0  
  
Give your solutions correct to 2 decimal places.

### 16. (3 marks)

A cyclist rides 250 m, correct to the nearest 10 m.  
  
The time taken is 32.4 seconds, correct to 1 decimal place.  
  
Work out the upper bound for the cyclist's average speed. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. Show your working.

### 17. (4 marks)

The number of bacteria, P, in a dish t hours after a test starts is  
  
P = 500 × 1.08ᵗ  
  
(a) Work out the number of bacteria after 6 hours.  
  
(b) Work out after how many whole hours the number of bacteria first goes above 2000.

### 18. (5 marks)

In triangle PQR, PQ = 8.5 cm, PR = 12.3 cm and angle QPR = 47°.  
  
(a) Work out the length of QR. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.  
  
(b) Work out the area of triangle PQR. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

### 19. (4 marks)

There are 60 students in a year group.  
  
35 study French, 28 study Spanish and 9 study neither language.  
  
One of the students who studies French is chosen at random.  
  
Work out the probability that this student also studies Spanish. Show your working.

### 20. (3 marks)

Two bottles are mathematically similar.  
  
The smaller bottle has height 12 cm and volume 320 cm³.  
  
The larger bottle has height 18 cm.  
  
Work out the volume of the larger bottle.

### 21. (3 marks)

The table gives information about the heights, h cm, of 100 plants.  
  
| Height (h cm) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0 < h ≤ 20 | 16 |
| 20 < h ≤ 30 | 22 |
| 30 < h ≤ 40 | 30 |
| 40 < h ≤ 60 | 24 |
| 60 < h ≤ 100 | 8 |
  
A histogram is drawn for this data. Use the frequency densities to work out an estimate for the number of plants with a height between 45 cm and 70 cm.

### 22. (4 marks)

Solve the simultaneous equations  
  
y = x² − 3x + 1  
y = 2x − 3  
  
Show your working.

### 23. (4 marks)

(a) Show that the equation x³ + 4x − 7 = 0 has a solution between x = 1 and x = 2.  
  
(b) Using the iteration formula  
  
xₙ₊₁ = ∛(7 − 4xₙ)  
  
with x₀ = 1.2, find the values of x₁, x₂ and x₃. Give your answers correct to 4 decimal places.

### 24. (4 marks)

OAB is a sector of a circle with centre O and radius 9 cm. Angle AOB = 70°.  
  
The chord AB is drawn. The segment is the region between the chord AB and the arc AB.  
  
Work out the area of the segment. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. Show your working.

## Mark scheme

### 1. Percentage change, growth and decay (3)

- Works out the sale price: 640 × 0.85 = £544
- Works out the new price: 544 × 1.15 = £625.60
- States Ali is not correct, with £625.60 (or 640 × 0.9775) as evidence

*Accept a multiplier argument: 0.85 × 1.15 = 0.9775, which is not 1, for the first two marks. The final mark needs a clear conclusion.*

### 2. Percentage change, growth and decay (3)

- Uses the multiplier 1.032
- 4500 × 1.032⁵
- £5267.58

*£720 interest (simple interest, giving £5220) scores 0. Accept £5267.578… for the method marks; the final mark needs £5267.58.*

### 3. Ratio (3)

- Works out the blue needed for all the yellow (1.8 ÷ 5 × 3 = 1.08 litres) or the yellow needed for all the blue (1.2 ÷ 3 × 5 = 2 litres)
- Identifies that the yellow paint runs out first (1.08 ≤ 1.2, or 2 > 1.8)
- 2.88 litres

*3 litres (using all the paint) scores 0. 3.2 litres (using all the blue) scores 1 for the method.*

### 4. Compound measures (3)

- Volume = 4 × 5 × 12 = 240 cm³
- Mass = 240 × 8.9 = 2136 g
- 2.136 kg

*2136 with no units, or in grams, scores 2.*

### 5. Averages and spread (3)

- Uses midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35
- Σ(midpoint × frequency) = 860 and divides by 40
- 21.5 minutes

*Using end points (10, 20, 30, 40) can score the second mark for dividing a consistent total by 40. Dividing by 4 scores at most 1.*

### 6. Indices and standard form (2)

- (5.97 × 10²⁴) ÷ (7.35 × 10²²)
- 81.2

*Accept 81.22… for the method mark; the answer mark needs 81.2.*

### 7. Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry (3)

- Uses tan: tan(ACB) = 7.4 ÷ 11.2
- ACB = tan⁻¹(7.4 ÷ 11.2)
- 33.5°

*56.5° (the angle at A) scores 2. A complete method via Pythagoras and sin or cos is fine.*

### 8. Area, volume and surface area (4)

- Uses V = πr²h for the cylinder: π × 6² × 15
- 540π or 1696.4… cm³
- Divides by the base area: 1696.4… ÷ 300
- 5.65 cm

*Using diameter 12 as the radius loses the first two marks but can score the third.*

### 9. Circle theorems (3)

- Forms the equation 3x + 10 + 2x + 20 = 180
- x = 30, so angle ABC = 100°
- Reason: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°

*The reason mark needs the words 'cyclic quadrilateral' (or equivalent) and 'opposite'.*

### 10. Direct and inverse proportion (3)

- Writes y = k ÷ x²
- k = 300
- y = 4.6875 (or 75/16)

*y = k/x (k = 60, y = 7.5) scores 0.*

### 11. Tree diagrams and conditional probability (3)

- Uses the second-pick probabilities out of 7: 5/8 × 3/7 or 3/8 × 5/7
- Adds both orders: 15/56 + 15/56
- 15/28 (or 30/56)

*With replacement (2 × 5/8 × 3/8 = 30/64) scores 1. Accept 0.536 (3 sf).*

### 12. Sequences (3)

- Second difference is 4, so the sequence starts 2n²
- Subtracts 2n² to leave 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (linear part n + 1)
- 2n² + n + 1

*4n² with a correct method for the rest scores 1.*

### 13. Straight-line graphs (3)

- Gradient of L = (−3 − 5) ÷ (6 − 2) = −2
- Perpendicular gradient = ½
- y = ½x − 1

*Accept any equivalent form, e.g. 2y = x − 2 or x − 2y − 2 = 0.*

### 14. Inequalities and rearranging formulae (4)

- Multiplies both sides by (x − 4): y(x − 4) = 3x + 2
- Expands: xy − 4y = 3x + 2
- Collects x terms on one side and factorises: x(y − 3) = 2 + 4y
- x = (4y + 2) / (y − 3)

*Accept equivalent forms such as x = (−4y − 2)/(3 − y).*

### 15. Linear and quadratic equations (3)

- Substitutes correctly into the quadratic formula: x = (7 ± √(49 + 48)) ÷ 6
- (7 ± √97) ÷ 6
- x = 2.81 and x = −0.47

*One correct solution scores 2. Completing the square is fine.*

### 16. Bounds, rounding and estimation (3)

- Uses the bounds 255 m and 32.35 s
- Upper bound of speed = 255 ÷ 32.35
- 7.88 m/s

*Using 32.45 s (giving 7.86) scores 1 for the distance bound.*

### 17. Percentage change, growth and decay (4)

- (a) 500 × 1.08⁶
- (a) 793 (accept 793.4…)
- (b) Tries values of t either side, e.g. t = 18 gives 1998 and t = 19 gives 2157.8…
- (b) 19 hours

*In (b), 18 hours scores 1 if 1998 is seen. Using logarithms is fine.*

### 18. Sine rule, cosine rule and area of a triangle (5)

- (a) QR² = 8.5² + 12.3² − 2 × 8.5 × 12.3 × cos 47°
- (a) QR² = 80.9…
- (a) QR = 9.00 cm (accept 8.996…)
- (b) ½ × 8.5 × 12.3 × sin 47°
- (b) 38.2 cm²

*Calculator in radians gives a wrong value: award method marks only.*

### 19. Venn diagrams and set notation (4)

- Works out that 60 − 9 = 51 study at least one language
- Number studying both = 35 + 28 − 51 = 12
- Uses French as the denominator
- 12/35

*12/60 (or 1/5) scores 2. A correct Venn diagram with 23, 12, 16, 9 scores the first two marks.*

### 20. Congruence and similarity (3)

- Linear scale factor 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5
- Volume scale factor 1.5³ = 3.375
- 1080 cm³

*480 cm³ (linear scale factor used) scores 1. 720 cm³ (area scale factor) scores 1.*

### 21. Histograms (3)

- Frequency densities for the two classes used: 24 ÷ 20 = 1.2 and 8 ÷ 40 = 0.2
- Uses the parts of the classes: 15 × 1.2 = 18 and 10 × 0.2 = 2
- 20 plants

*Proportion method (15/20 × 24 + 10/40 × 8) is fine for full marks.*

### 22. Simultaneous equations (4)

- Eliminates y: x² − 3x + 1 = 2x − 3
- Rearranges to x² − 5x + 4 = 0
- Solves: (x − 1)(x − 4) = 0, so x = 1 or x = 4
- x = 1, y = −1 and x = 4, y = 5

*The last mark needs both pairs, correctly paired.*

### 23. Functions and iteration (4)

- (a) Substitutes both values: x = 1 gives −2 and x = 2 gives 9
- (a) States there is a change of sign, so there is a solution between 1 and 2
- (b) x₁ = 1.3006
- (b) x₂ = 1.2159 and x₃ = 1.2879

*In (b), allow error carried forward from a wrong x₁ for x₂ and x₃ if the formula is used correctly.*

### 24. Area, volume and surface area (4)

- Area of sector = 70/360 × π × 9² = 49.48…
- Area of triangle OAB = ½ × 9 × 9 × sin 70° = 38.05…
- Subtracts: 49.48… − 38.05…
- 11.4 cm²

*Accept 11.42… for the method marks.*
